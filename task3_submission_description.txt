This report presents my Real-World Dataset Analysis Project, an end-to-end analysis of 6,433 genuine New York City taxi trips recorded in March 2019. The data comes from the NYC Taxi and Limousine Commission trip records, and I documented its source and checksum so the work can be verified. I chose it for its clear business context, mixed numeric, categorical, time and geographic variables, and real data-quality problems.

I explored the structure, data types, missing values, duplicates, distributions and relationships, and found 186 missing cells, no duplicates, text timestamps and suspicious zero-passenger and zero-distance trips. Cleaning was fully logged and validated with assertions: 63 rows (1 percent) were removed for invalid distance, duration, date window or impossible speed, missing categories were labelled Unknown, and genuine outliers were kept and handled with medians, trimmed means and rank-based statistics.

The exploratory analysis used Pandas, NumPy and SciPy, with a regression model, non-parametric tests, effect sizes and a bootstrap confidence interval. Key findings: 82 percent of pickups are in Manhattan, demand peaks at 18:00, and median speed stays near 9 miles per hour through the working day. Fare follows a base charge plus distance and time components, with a held-out R-squared of 0.94. Cash tips are never recorded, so tipping was analysed on card trips only. The apparent tip gap between yellow and green cabs is largely explained by location and zero-tip trips.

Fourteen visualisations and a structured Jupyter notebook document the method, interpretations, conclusions, recommendations and limitations. The complete project is in my GitHub repository.
